Why it exists

The schedule was never
the hard part.

Most project managers I have worked with know exactly how their job should be sequenced. What stops them is the software. MS Project and P6 were built for planners, and everyone else is expected to spend weeks learning menus before they can answer a simple question: when do we finish, and what is driving it?

So the schedule gets handed to someone else, goes stale after the second month, and the client gets a bar chart nobody on site believes. The Scheduler takes the software out of the way. You think in work, sequence and progress. It does the critical path arithmetic and tells you, in plain language, what it found.

What it does

From the first activity
to the monthly report.

Every function follows the way a project actually runs: plan it, agree it, update it, explain it.

Build

Work in your own terms

Work packages and activities. Durations from quantities and production rates, with every assumption flagged until it is confirmed.

Logic

Connect the work

Finish-to-start, start-to-start, finish-to-finish and start-to-finish, with working-day lags. Logic loops are rejected before they cause damage.

Critical path

Know what drives the finish

Early and late dates, float and the driving activities, explained in plain language instead of a column of numbers.

Gantt

Move work, then decide

Drag unstarted work on the Gantt. The change comes back as a proposal you review before anything is applied.

Baseline

Agree the plan, then measure against it

Save the agreed programme as a baseline. Forecast slippage shows against it on the Gantt and in every report.

Progress

One honest update per period

Set a status date, record actuals, percent complete and remaining duration, then commit the update once. Out-of-sequence work is flagged.

Health

A schedule that checks itself

Negative float, missing links, long activities, unexplained lags and progress shortfalls, each with the reason it was flagged and the next action.

Look-ahead

Ready for the site meeting

Two- and four-week look-aheads of unfinished work, printable for the Monday coordination meeting.

Reporting

The client report, already written

A management report with forecast completion, margin against the required finish, milestones, attention items and what changed.

Excel

A workbook people can read

Formatted export with the schedule, predecessors, actuals, baseline, look-aheads, findings and calendar.

MS Project

Works with what the client uses

Import a schedule from MS Project XML, Excel or CSV. Export XML for the client, with every information loss disclosed before you accept it.

Bilingual

English and Spanish

Switch language at any time. The interface, findings and reports follow. Your project data stays exactly as you entered it.

Questions

Before you buy.

Standard or Pro?

If you work on one computer, Standard is enough. If you open the same project on a laptop, an office PC and a tablet, or you do not want to depend on remembering backups, choose Pro.

Do I need MS Project or Primavera P6?

No. The Scheduler calculates the critical path itself. If your client works in MS Project, you can export an XML file they can open.

Can I bring in a schedule I already have?

Yes. Import from MS Project XML, Excel or CSV. You review the dates, warnings and anything that could not be carried across before you accept the import.

Where is my project data stored?

In Standard, in your browser on your device; nothing is stored on our servers, so export a JSON backup regularly. In Pro, your projects are also saved to the cloud and sync automatically to every device you sign in on. Either way, keep your original documents separately.

Does it work without internet?

Signing in and connecting your licence need internet. Once Standard is open, the scheduling workspace works offline. Pro needs a connection to save to the cloud and sync between devices.

How does the licence work?

Buy the edition you want on Gumroad, then sign in with the same email you used to purchase and enter your licence key once. Both licences run for 365 days from purchase.

Is it right for very large programmes?

It is built for the projects most PMs actually run. It does not do resource levelling or multiple calendars per activity. For a 5,000-activity EPC programme with a dedicated planning team, stay with P6.
